GSAP SplitText breaks a DOM element into characters, words, and lines so you can animate typography with precise control. A character reveal staggers each letter into place—ideal for hero copy, editorial intros, and scroll-driven storytelling when paired with ScrollTrigger.

SplitText can wrap words in masks, letting you slide or clip content without splitting your markup by hand. Word-level masks pair naturally with staggered tweens: each word moves on its own timeline while ScrollTrigger scrubs progress as you move through the section.

Beyond position and opacity, SplitText targets are plain elements—filters, blur, and scale all work per word. Stagger spreads the motion across the phrase so the paragraph feels rhythmic instead of monolithic, which reads better on long body copy like this.

You can split by characters and words in one pass, then animate both sets for layered motion. Smart splitting respects nested tags and responsive reflow; autoSplit re-runs when the layout changes so line breaks stay accurate after fonts load and the viewport resizes.

Line-based splits are sensitive to font metrics—always wait for document.fonts.ready before measuring. Animate lines with staggered from-tweens and scrollTrigger on the tween, not inside onEnter, so initial styles apply before the first paint and the reveal does not flash finished.

Line masks clip overflowing content so reveals feel like curtains rising on each row. Combine words and lines when you need per-line masking with word-level nuance inside long paragraphs. Revert splits on cleanup to restore accessible, semantic HTML when animations unmount.
